## 2. What already works

- Every venue named carries a specific, checkable fact (a founding year, an address, a design credit, a named booking), not a generic descriptor.
- Patrick Miller's 1983 founding and unbroken Friday-only format is corroborated across every editorial source read (Time Out 2018 and 2025, Mexico City's own tourism authority, Trippin, Tripadvisor), giving the guide a genuine "still running on its original format" anchor the way Lux Frágil anchored Lisbon and Motion anchored Bristol, without needing a closure story to make the point.
- M.N.Roy's former-Communist-Party-headquarters history and Chic by Accident interior design credit come from The Spaces, an architecture/design trade publication, read in full rather than a listing snippet, and place the club in the same international "cult clubs" feature as Lux Frágil and Berghain, an independent cross-check of the guide's own editorial judgment.
- Fünk Club and Yu Yu Cine Club's founding details, bookings and current programming come from DJ Mag's 2024 feature on the city's underground, read in full, the same evidentiary tier used for Lisbon's Musicbox and Ministerium sections.
- Perreo Millennial's July/August 2026 closure is a genuinely current, dated story (confirmed via Resident Advisor's own news feed), found the same way as Bristol's Motion and Lisbon's Musicbox: by checking for a live status story before defaulting to pure history. It is honestly kept out of the venue table, since it never held a fixed address, rather than forced in as a fourth "club."
- Two Boiler Room embeds (Turbo Sonidero, 2025; Nic Fanciulli, 2018) were found by web research, cross-checked against the Selector catalogue by artist name, and confirmed against each video's own YouTube oEmbed title.
- Genre framing follows `WRITING.md`'s "not every article is about breaks/jungle/dnb" rule: the guide covers disco, house, techno, cumbia sonidero and reggaeton/perreo, Mexico City's own sounds, with no forced UK bass/jungle angle.

## 3. Priority revisions

**Applied in this pass (humanizer pass, 2026-09-25):**

- **Major, factual — an unsupported superlative about M.N.Roy.** The original draft's Answer section claimed M.N.Roy had "become one of the most photographed clubs in Latin America," a superlative with no source behind it. The Spaces calls it "one of Mexico City's hottest nightspots," a narrower and sourced claim. Corrected to "has drawn international design coverage for its interior," which is what the sourcing actually supports.
- **Major, factual — a mismatched attribution on the perreo/DJ Mag claim.** The FAQ answer originally credited "DJ Mag's own reporting" with a direct editorial claim about perreo's growth. The actual source is a quote from a named booker (Josta) inside DJ Mag's 2024 feature, not the magazine's own editorial voice. Corrected to "DJ Mag's 2024 feature... quoted local bookers describing..." so the claim is attributed to its actual speaker.
- **Major, factual — an overclaimed Boiler Room/sonidero connection.** The original draft stated sonideros "have their own dedicated Boiler Room strand, SYSTEM," implying SYSTEM exists specifically for sonidero music. SYSTEM is Boiler Room's wider Latin American city series; sonideros are one of several sounds it has featured, including a specific "Sonidero Special" episode. Rewritten to describe SYSTEM accurately as a regional series that "regularly gives sonidero DJs the same billing as touring international names," rather than claiming it is sonidero-dedicated.
- **Minor, voice — a formulaic sentence reused from the Lisbon guide published earlier the same day.** "House and techno aren't the only sound [city] has exported" appeared as the transition into both this guide's Turbo Sonidero paragraph and Lisbon's Buraka Som Sistema paragraph, verbatim except for the city name — a template phrase rather than a sentence composed for this city. Rewritten as "Not every club night in the city runs on imported house and techno," a different construction making the same point.
- **Minor, voice — a repeated "map" metaphor.** "Putting the city on international bookers' maps" (introduction) and "helping put Mexico City's underground on the international map" (M.N.Roy/Fünk section) repeated the same cliché twice in one draft. Reworded both instances to avoid the repetition and the cliché itself.
- **Minor — an awkward self-referential phrase describing the DJ.** "Mexican DJ Roberto Devesa, who performs and is known by the same name" was unclear about what "the same name" referred to. Rewritten as "who performs under the same name as the club."
- **Minor — a wordy closing clause.** "Entry costs a few dozen pesos, drinks are cheap, and neither fact has done anything to dim its reputation" was tightened to two direct sentences, removing the "done anything to dim" filler construction.

## 6. Coverage gaps

- **Major, acknowledged, not fixed in this pass.** The "best clubs in Mexico City now" table names four venues: Patrick Miller, M.N.Roy, Fünk Club, Yu Yu Cine Club. RA's own current listings also name Sunday Sunday, Departamento, La Santa and Antro Juan as popular current clubs, none researched to the same one-venue depth, the same shape of gap Bristol's and Lisbon's reviews flagged for their own tables.
- **Major, acknowledged.** No licensed photograph exists on Wikimedia Commons for any of the four required venues themselves; all three images used show the surrounding neighbourhoods (the Zócalo, a Roma Norte street, a Condesa street) rather than the clubs' own buildings, and captions are explicit about this.
- **Minor.** M.N.Roy's exact opening date could not be confirmed beyond "the early 2010s" (see §4).
- **Minor.** Neither Boiler Room embed is tied to a named venue, the same limitation Bristol's two embeds had; both are presented as city-wide, not venue-specific, evidence.
- **Minor.** No German or French translation exists yet for this page.
