Run 2026-09-25, before the build was reported as finished, per `ARTICLE-PRODUCTION-WORKFLOW.md` §6. Second city-club guide to get the humanizer/fact-check/SEO pass before a "done" report rather than after, following Bristol's example earlier the same day.

## 1. Verdict

**Ready.** No remaining blocker. Coverage gaps (below) are real limitations, not defects, left to the owner's call.

## 2. What already works

- Every venue named carries a specific, checkable fact (opening date, address, a ranking, a closure, a named booking), not a generic descriptor.
- Musicbox's September 2025 closure is a real, dated, multi-source news story, not colour: Liveurope, ICNS.lx and a Rock Bar Legends piece dated 5 September 2026 all independently confirm the closure, and DJ Mag's own March 2026 feature carries a direct quote from Príncipe label DJ Marfox on what the club meant to him. This is the same shape of current-status anchor that Motion gave the Bristol guide, found the same way: by checking for a live story before defaulting to pure history, per the owner's instruction.
- Ministerium's Konda Records Afro-house nights and Lux Frágil's history both rest on named, checkable facts (RA's own repeated venue description for the opening date, Portuguese Wikipedia's cited history for Frágil's origins, direct DJ Marfox quotes for Ministerium's current sound) rather than an assertion that Lisbon "is" a house-and-techno city.
- Two Boiler Room video embeds (Buraka Som Sistema, 2013; Parris, 2019) were found by web research first, cross-checked against the Selector catalogue by artist name, then confirmed against each video's own YouTube oEmbed title, the same method used for Bristol. The Parris embed is unusually well anchored: the oEmbed title itself names the venue (Village Underground Lisboa), so the guide can tie the video to a specific room rather than leaving it venue-less as Bristol's two embeds had to be.
- The guide is honest about the scene's current pressure (Musicbox, Outra Cena and Lounge all closed since 2024) rather than presenting Lisbon's clubbing as a static, unchanging highlight reel — directly following the pasted brief's instruction to report coverage gaps and trends honestly, including a volume trend the owner flagged (this page's own keyword volume fell over the trailing three months, per the wave-3 dossier note, though the page does not cite that figure since it is a research-stage signal, not a reader-facing fact).
- Genre framing follows `WRITING.md`'s "not every article is about breaks/jungle/dnb" rule: the guide covers house, techno, Afro-house and kuduro, Lisbon's own sounds, with no forced UK bass/jungle angle, aside, or Bandcamp line.

## 3. Priority revisions

**Applied in this pass (humanizer pass, 2026-09-25):**

- **Major, factual — a false connection between Lux Frágil and kuduro.** The original draft's transition into the Buraka Som Sistema paragraph read "Lux's own sound owes something to a wider Lisbon export," implying Lux books kuduro. It does not; Lux runs house and techno, and Buraka Som Sistema is a separate act on a separate embed. Rewritten to "House and techno aren't the only sound Lisbon has exported," which introduces the kuduro paragraph without misattributing it to Lux.
- **Major, factual — an unverified claim that Buraka Som Sistema "split up."** Checked: the group did not split up. They announced an indefinite hiatus in August 2015, played a final Lisbon show on 1 July 2016, and reunited in 2025 after a ten-year break (Wikipedia, Billboard, OkayAfrica, Remezcla, Enchufada's own Instagram). The draft now states the hiatus and 2025 reunion accurately instead of the incorrect "split up."
- **Major, factual — a misquote of DJ Marfox.** The original draft paraphrased DJ Mag's Musicbox-closure quote loosely ("the closure felt personal... the DJ, artist and person he is now"), changing both the reported emotion (DJ Mag's word is "strange," not "personal") and the wording of the quote itself. Corrected to quote DJ Mag's actual word ("strange") and a short, accurate fragment of the real quote ("the best DJ, the best artist, the best person"), kept under 15 words per the copyright limit on quoted material.
- **Major, factual — an unsupported claim about Outra Cena's fundraiser purpose.** The draft stated the fundraising compilation was "to cover legal costs," a detail that traces to a different venue's (Planeta Manas's) legal-cost appeal in a low-quality blog source, not to anything RA's own reporting says about Outra Cena. RA states only that proceeds "support the club." Corrected to remove the unsupported "legal costs" specific.
- **Major, factual — an attribution error on the rent/redevelopment claim.** The FAQ answer on the scene's struggles originally credited "Portuguese press coverage" with tying closures to rising rents and redevelopment. That framing and evidence come from DJ Mag (a UK trade publication), not Portuguese-language press read in this pass. Corrected to attribute it to DJ Mag directly.
- **Major, geographic accuracy — the "Where to go out" neighbourhood map.** The original draft grouped Lux Frágil with the "Cais do Sodré and Baixa riverfront" cluster. Lux's own address (Cais da Pedra, postal code 1950) and Lisbon Lux's own neighbourhood tag place it at the eastern edge of the waterfront in Alfama, near Santa Apolónia, not in the central Cais do Sodré/Baixa cluster with Ministerium and Kremlin. Rewritten with an accurate three-area map: Ministerium in Baixa, Kremlin and Pink Street in Cais do Sodré/Santos, and Lux Frágil alone further east in Alfama.
- **Minor, voice — an unsupported "more-watched" framing.** A sentence claiming Parris's Boiler Room set was "one of the more-watched" broadcasts from Village Underground Lisboa compared only two videos' view counts (21,961 vs. 14,781), a trivial and slightly inflated comparison. Replaced with a plainer, unquantified sentence about international bookings reaching the venue.
- **Minor, rule compliance — self-referential commentary about the guide's own honesty.** Two sentences (in the introduction and the closing Answer) described the guide itself as reporting "honestly" or "rather than papering over" its own coverage gaps. `AGENTS.md` §4 prohibits editorial or implementation commentary in published copy; this is exactly that, aimed at the article rather than the reader. Both sentences were cut; the underlying facts (that several venues have closed) are stated plainly instead.
- **Minor — a not-especially-necessary staged phrase.** "Kremlin... has had a rougher road to its current form" was softened to the more direct "closed once before reaching its current form."
- **Minor — a PAA-shaped FAQ heading tightened to match real search language.** "What is the biggest or most famous club in Lisbon?" (a compound, invented question) was replaced with the actual PAA phrasing observed twice in this session's live SERP reads: "What is the best nightclub in Lisbon, Portugal?"

## 6. Coverage gaps

- **Major, acknowledged, not fixed in this pass.** The "best clubs in Lisbon now" table names four venues: Lux Frágil, Ministerium, Kremlin, Village Underground Lisboa. DJ Mag's own reporting names a much larger DIY ecosystem (Those Who Dance, Higher Ground, Vago, Sala Lisa, Damas, Planeta Manas, 8 Marvila) that was not researched to the same one-venue depth, matching the same shape of gap Bristol's review flagged for its own three-venue table.
- **Major, acknowledged.** No licensed photograph exists for Ministerium Club's own interior or facade, or for Kremlin's building; the Ministerium section illustrates the square it occupies rather than the club itself, and Kremlin runs on text alone.
- **Minor.** The Guardian's 2014 ranking of Lux Frágil is reported via two independent secondary citations rather than the primary article, which could not be opened directly this session (see §4).
- **Minor.** Musicbox's exact closure date (15 September 2025) rests on a single source (Rock Bar Legends); the article states the month, not the day, to avoid over-precision on a single-sourced detail.
- **Minor.** No German or French translation exists yet for this page.
