# German electronic music: editorial review

Review date: 15 September 2026

## Verdict

Ready to publish after final rendered-page QA. The article has one clear job: explain how German electronic music developed through distinct cities, institutions and scenes. It does not collapse that history into techno or turn into an artist ranking.

## Editorial and factual checks

- The opening answers the primary query directly.
- Cologne studio composition, Düsseldorf electronic pop, West Berlin sequencer music, Frankfurt club infrastructure and post-Wall Berlin techno remain distinct.
- The article states clearly that techno originated in Detroit and avoids a German invention myth.
- Dates, institutions, releases and scene claims were checked against the source ledger in `german-electronic-music-research.md`.
- Precise or contested claims are qualified or linked near the relevant passage.
- The prose avoids em dashes, inflated scene-setting and repeated conclusions.
- Visible brand spelling is consistently `thecatrave`.

## SEO and structure checks

- This is a new URL, so there is no Search Console preservation inventory.
- One H1, semantic heading order, direct-answer block, compact contents and five useful FAQ items are present.
- The broad intent is supported by German techno terminology without replacing the article's national-history focus.
- Club rankings, current events, DJ lists and production tutorials remain excluded.
- Article, breadcrumb and visible FAQ structured data match the page.

## Media and conversion checks

- Each historical listening example appears next to the passage it supports.
- Images are licensed, local, responsive and separated from embeds by prose.
- The original city-route graphic has an accessible mobile alternative and downloadable PNG.
- The final Bandcamp block is the commercial route; article examples are not disguised promotions.
- Media sources, licences and embed IDs are recorded in `media/german-electronic.json`.

## Known limitations

- The original 1974 *Phaedra* master has no reliable exact embed, so the article uses the official release history rather than a misleading player.
- Basic Channel's original two-track release is embedded as a Spotify album because Spotify does not expose the requested recording as a single-track iframe.
- Live third-party playback can change after publication and should be rechecked if a platform reports an error.
