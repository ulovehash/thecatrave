# DJ Mag Top 100 Festivals: search demand and article priorities

Checked 15 September 2026. Ranking source: DJ Mag Top 100 Festivals 2026.
Demand source: Google Keyword Planner, worldwide, all languages, Google,
September 2025–August 2026. Ahrefs was not used.

The Google Ads account has no active campaign, so Keyword Planner exposes
buckets rather than dependable exact figures. The exported values are bucket
representatives: 5,000 means 1K–10K, 50,000 means 10K–100K, and so on. They
are useful for triage, not for pretending that every term in a bucket has the
same volume.

Short or generic names are marked `collision`: their raw volume includes
other meanings and must not drive the queue until the owner supplies a
festival-specific export. Franchise editions are normally sections or updates
to an existing parent guide unless their own SERP proves that a separate page
is wanted.

### Expand existing guides instead of creating a new article first

- Tomorrowland: Tomorrowland Winter; The Magic of Tomorrowland.
- Ultra: Ultra Europe, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Australia, Buenos Aires and South
  Africa.
- EDC: Orlando, Mexico and Thailand until a supplied export demonstrates
  enough distinct informational intent for separate pages.
- Untold: Untold Dubai.
- Creamfields: Creamfields Chile.
- S2O: S2O Taiwan.
- Defected: Croatia and Malta should share a parent guide if we enter that
  cluster.

### Do not prioritise from the raw head-term volume

- `AMF` (100K–1M), `Time Warp` (100K–1M), `DGTL` (10K–100K), `Parklife`
  (10K–100K), `Panorama Festival` (10K–100K), `Nature One` (10K–100K),
  `Soundstorm` (10K–100K) and `Electric Love` (10K–100K) have material query
  collisions. Measure a festival-qualified form and inspect the SERP first.
- Glastonbury's full phrase appears as 10K–100K here, while the earlier
  `glastonbury` export was 193,000. This is why the buckets are a triage layer,
  not a replacement for the owner's keyword research.
