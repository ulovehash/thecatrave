# Bass music guide editorial review

Review date: 2026-08-31

## 1. Verdict

**Ready after revisions.**

The draft has a clear editorial argument, answers the primary search intent early and avoids the two weak formats dominating competitors: a generic subgenre list and an unrelated production/frequency guide. No structural rewrite is required. Exact track verification, selective tightening and media commissioning remain before layout.

## 2. What already works

- The opening answers `what is bass music` without treating the term as one universally agreed genre.
- The draft separates musical practices that predate the label from the late-2000s and early-2010s rise of a recognisable UK umbrella usage.
- Miami bass is treated as an independent American history rather than an offshoot of UK dubstep.
- UK and US meanings are compared through actual institutions, cities and scenes.
- Footwork and gqom retain their own local identities instead of being absorbed into a false global bass family tree.
- The infrastructure section gives the article an argument competitors largely lack: systems, dubplates, radio, shops, labels and rooms help create genre meaning.
- Exact listening examples are planned beside relevant prose.
- The contemporary thecatrave mix has an editorial reason to appear and is not presented as part of the historical canon.
- The conclusion answers whether the umbrella remains useful rather than ending with a generic future prediction.

## 3. Priority revisions

### Major: verify every exact track and embed

Every final record needs:

- correct artist and title;
- release year and original context;
- exact Spotify, YouTube or SoundCloud embed;
- a verified reason it demonstrates the surrounding claim;
- a unique accessible iframe title.

No playlist may replace an exact track without an explicit explanation.

### Major: convert the artist section into a non-duplicative recap

`Bass music artists and records that changed the culture` currently repeats records already discussed earlier. Keep its keyword and discovery value, but publish it as a compact visual listening index that links back to the relevant sections. Do not render a second copy of the same twelve players.

### Major: commission the article-specific extended playlist

The existing thecatrave playlists have not yet been checked track by track against the approved narrative. Either verify one existing playlist or build a dedicated Bass Music listening playlist. Do not choose by title alone.

### Major: verify the modern examples

The present-day section needs a small, defensible selection representing concurrent scenes rather than whichever records are currently fashionable. Bakey is supported by recent specialist coverage. Add no more than three further current examples after source and listening review.

### Minor: reduce repeated umbrella disclaimers

The draft correctly qualifies the term, but several later sections repeat that the category is unstable. During line edit, retain the strongest formulation in each section and remove sentences that merely restate the thesis.

### Minor: check whether `Bass music artists` must remain an H2

If the compact recap does not contain enough distinct editorial value, retitle it as an H3 within `Types of bass music`. Preserve the semantic concept naturally in copy and metadata rather than protecting a weak heading for a low-volume keyword.

## 5. SEO preservation and semantic coverage

This is a new page, so there is no legacy search visibility to preserve.

### Primary language present

- `what is bass music`: H1 and opening answer;
- `bass music`: title, H1, deck and body;
- `bass music definition`: visible definition label;
- `bass music meaning`: addressed by the regional-meaning H2;
- `bass music genre`: answered in the definition and FAQ;
- `types of bass music`: exact H2;
- `bass music artists`: exact H2, subject to the editorial value test above;
- `UK bass music`: exact H3;
- `bass music genres`: natural body wording and listening-map context.

### Intent containment passes

The draft does not expand into:

- bass guitar;
- frequency science;
- production tools;
- speaker tests;
- sample packs;
- bass-boosted song discovery;
- a generic best-songs ranking.

### FAQ passes with one condition

The five questions remain within the approved intent and can be marked up with visible FAQ plus matching structured data. Their published answers should be tightened slightly so they do not reproduce full body paragraphs.

## 6. Coverage gaps

- Exact verification of the article-specific track route.
- A trustworthy platform URL for every player.
- Final selection of two or three current artists beyond Bakey.
- Verification of one extended playlist and one historical or scene-defining mix.
- Final source for any sentence that implies a first, origin or decisive turning point.

### Valuable but not blocking

- One original short comment from a UK DJ, producer, label or promoter about what `bass music` means in practice.
- One North American participant voice showing a different use of the term.
- A first-person paragraph from thecatrave about why multi-genre sets make the umbrella useful while digging and mixing.

## 7. Cuts or merges

- Do not add a separate frequency or production section.
- Do not add a large festival directory.
- Do not add an annual timeline after 2010.
- Do not present all candidate genres as descendants in one family tree.
- Merge duplicated artist descriptions between the history, listening map and recap.
- Keep only one strong explanation of the difference between broad `UK bass music` and narrower `UK bass` usage.
- The listening recap must not duplicate players already embedded in the article.

## 8. Media actions

### Original linkable visual

Commission `Bass Music: One Term, Different Meanings` as a regional comparison rather than a family tree. It should compare:

- UK broad bass continuum;
- narrower UK bass/post-dubstep usage;
- Miami bass;
- Los Angeles/West Coast bass;
- North American festival bass;
- cross-border club programming.

It must say what each usage includes, what infrastructure shaped it and what the category may conceal. Provide a high-resolution PNG, an SVG and an accessible HTML equivalent.

### Archival evidence

Priorities:

- Jamaican or British sound-system photograph;
- dubplate or cutting-house artefact;
- Night Slugs, FWD>>, DMZ or related late-2000s flyer/club artefact;
- Miami bass flyer, car-system or local party artefact;
- Low End Theory or Smog photograph/flyer;
- one contemporary cross-genre DJ image or booth photograph.

Do not reuse the same hero artefacts from the Jungle, Breakbeat or UK Electronic Music articles without a specific editorial reason.

### Listening rhythm

Maintain `text → image → text` and separate every image from every embed with meaningful prose. No figure may touch a player, playlist, video collection or Bandcamp stripe.
