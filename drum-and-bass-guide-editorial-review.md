## 1. Verdict

**Ready after revisions.** No factual blocker. The spine, intent and voice are right. Three Major revisions before layout: (a) name more exact records in the prose, (b) give Bristol its due, (c) add one concrete paragraph each to the Metalheadz and atmospheric sections so depth matches the other guides. ## 2. What already works (do not damage)

- **The framing.** "Drum and bass is the music jungle turned into" answers the primary question in the first sentence and sets up the whole plan's asymmetry. The definition block is a clean, quotable paragraph with the tempo, the origin, the DnB spelling and the jungle relationship.
- **The prologue is correctly capped.** Three paragraphs, ends by handing the racial/class/General Levy argument to the jungle guide. It does not re-argue jungle's story. This is the single most important structural decision and it holds.
- **The dubstep contrast** in the sound section (174 full-tempo vs 140 half-time, same lineage, opposite solutions) is accurate, useful and a natural link.
- **Uncertainty is handled, not smoothed.** "There is no clean date for the split, and any account that gives you one is tidying up." "Who first used it in print is not settled." No forced origin myth.
- **SEO language sits naturally.** dnb / dnb music / what is drum and bass / drum and bass bpm / subgenres / artists / liquid / neurofunk / techstep all present without stuffing. Title, meta and H1 share one intent and are not boilerplate copies.
- **The ending synthesises** ("the split with jungle... has, in practice, closed") rather than stopping before the FAQ.

## 3. Priority revisions

**Major 1 — name the records.** The workflow wants concrete, audible detail. The draft names artists and labels but few tracks. Add, in prose (the media blocks are separate):
- Sound section: one named two-step example and one named rolling example so the reader can hear the distinction.
- Metalheadz section: at least one specific record (e.g. a Photek or Doc Scott 12", or Goldie's "Inner City Life") with one line on what it sounds like.
- Atmospheric section: keep "Music" (1993); add one Roni Size / Reprazent track name.
- Global section: name Marky & XRS "LK" (2002) explicitly, not just "his crossover around 2002".

**Major 2 — Bristol.** The draft mentions "Bristol and liquid lineages" once. Bristol is central to D&B, not a footnote: Roni Size, Krust, Die, Suv and DJ Krust's Full Cycle, the More Rockers / V Recordings axis, and the fact that the Mercury-winning act was a Bristol collective. Add two to three sentences, most naturally in the atmospheric/Roni Size section or as a short bridge before "How drum and bass stopped being British". Do not build a separate Bristol section (bass-music and jungle guides carry the wider Bristol story; link where useful).

**Major 3 — depth in sections 3 and 4.** Both are currently two paragraphs. One more concrete paragraph each: for Metalheadz, the Sunday Sessions as a testing room and what "dubplate culture" meant here specifically (short, link to jungle `#pioneers` and to breakbeat for the technique); for the atmospheric line, the Speed crowd and how "intelligent drum and bass" was a real contemporary label that aged badly, i.e. terminology history, which is exactly what the workflow asks a guide to do.

**Minor** — "By 2000 the melodic branch had a name of its own, liquid" reads slightly flat; tie it to a record or a room. "a crowd that did not applaud politely" is a nice line, keep it. The subgenre bullet list will become `articleTable`; keep the four-column shape (term / when / what / relationship) used on the other guides.
