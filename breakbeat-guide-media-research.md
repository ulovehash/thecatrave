# Breakbeat Guide: Media Research and Listening Plan

**Status:** research draft for approval. Do not insert into the live article yet.

## Editorial principle

This article is about breakbeat as a genre, its scenes and its history. Media should document people, places, labels, clubs, records and regional variations. It should not turn the article into a drum-programming tutorial.

The recurring sequence should be:

1. Explain the scene or period.
2. Show one artifact that belongs to it.
3. Give the reader one playable example.
4. Explain what that example demonstrates historically or culturally.

## Player hierarchy

Use two kinds of players:

- **Feature player:** a responsive 16:9 YouTube embed used only when the video itself adds historical or visual information.
- **Listening example:** a compact Spotify, SoundCloud or Bandcamp track embed placed beside or immediately after the relevant style description.

Recommended total:

- 2 feature videos;
- 6 compact style examples;
- 1 curated Spotify playlist;
- 1 thecatrave SoundCloud mix;
- 1 compact Bandcamp CTA/player.

All players remain visible and playable without an extra reveal button. Below-the-fold iframes should use lazy loading.

## Proposed media sequence

### 1. Opening / definition

**Media:** no rhythm diagram and no production visual.

Use a concise editorial collage or hero composition made from scene artifacts: a cropped early rave flyer, a record label, a sampler detail and a contemporary club photograph. Its purpose is to announce a history of the genre rather than explain a drum pattern.

### 2. Funk and hip-hop origins

**Visual candidates:**

- DJ Kool Herc at the turntables: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Herc_on_the_Wheels_of_Steel.JPG
- Grandmaster Flash / early hip-hop performance or equipment artifact from an established photo archive.
- A restrained four-record artifact strip for The Winstons, James Brown, Incredible Bongo Band and Lyn Collins. Use the sleeves as small documentary objects, not as a full-width wallpaper.

**Playable example:** one compact playlist containing:

1. The Winstons — Amen, Brother
2. James Brown — Funky Drummer
3. Incredible Bongo Band — Apache
4. Lyn Collins — Think (About It)

This is an origins playlist, not four separate large embeds.

### 3. Sampling technology

**Visual candidates:**

- E-mu SP-1200;
- Akai S950 or S1000;
- Akai MPC60;
- period studio photograph showing one of these machines in use.

Use one equipment image only, displayed as an annotated artifact card rather than a large product gallery. The image should support the historical point about access, memory limitations and hands-on sequencing without opening a production tutorial.

### 4. Early UK rave and breakbeat hardcore

**Primary visual candidate:** Telepathy & Freedom, October 1991. The flyer documents a bill including The Prodigy, Carl Cox, Grooverider, LTJ Bukem and Jumping Jack Frost:

https://www.phatmedia.co.uk/flyers/event/telepathy-freedom-october-91

**Alternative / supporting artifacts:**

- Ultim8 1992 flyer already stored in the project;
- Rave Archive UK flyer collection: https://rave-archive.com/flyers/
- original cassette or tape-pack scan from Rave Archive UK.

**Primary listening example:** SL2 — DJs Take Control (1991)

Spotify: https://open.spotify.com/track/0sE1Ta0tO8eWIjlVPaWfse

**Optional feature video:** The Prodigy — Charly (Official Video)

YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cSTBFZ-To2E

Use SL2 as the direct genre example. Use Charly only if the accompanying paragraph discusses crossover, television-era imagery and the friction between underground rave and chart success.

### 5. Scene infrastructure: pirate radio, record shops, dubplates and tape packs

**Visual candidates:**

- original pirate-radio cassette scan or studio photograph from the Radiokillaz archive: https://radiokillaz.co.uk/pirate-radio-archive.html
- a Rave Archive UK tape-pack artifact;
- the existing dubplate-cutting photograph in the project;
- the reverse of a rave flyer listing ticket outlets and record shops: https://www.thewire.co.uk/galleries/rave-flyers-1991-1993

This should be a compact three-object editorial strip. It visually demonstrates the distribution network without adding another large player.

### 6. Florida breaks / Orlando sound

**Primary visual pairing:**

- The Return of AAHZ flyer, 7 September 1991: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Return_of_Aahz_on_Saturday,_September_7,_1991.jpg
- Beacham Theatre interior in March 1991: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beacham_Theatre_ceiling_and_columns_in_March_1991.jpg

This pairing is stronger than a generic American rave photograph because it connects the regional sound to an identifiable club and event.

**Alternative artifact:** Rave New World, Cocoa Beach, Florida, 11 December 1992, featuring The Prodigy, Moby, Richie Hawtin and local selectors:

https://www.theprodigyontour.com/1992/1992-12-11-rave-new-world-thee-areena-cocoa-beach-usa/

**Primary listening example:** DJ Icey — This Is How My Drummer Drums

Reason: a 1997/98 Florida-breaks production with documented Billboard dance-chart activity, more historically useful than choosing a recent DJ Icey track simply because it is easier to stream.

Fallback if a single-track embed cannot be resolved: DJ Icey's Spotify artist player or the album *Generate*.

Verified Spotify album fallback containing the track: https://open.spotify.com/album/0Z0NpVQKbDsm0vlUNhkzAB

### 7. Big beat

**Primary artifact:** Cream presents The Chemical Brothers live at Nation, Liverpool, October 1995:

https://www.phatmedia.co.uk/flyers/event/cream-flyer6

**Primary listening example:** The Chemical Brothers — Chemical Beats

Spotify: https://open.spotify.com/track/2uFngE6ePszeJV3Cbtrfpc

This example should accompany the explanation of big beat as a related crossover scene, not be presented as the definition of all breakbeat.

### 8. Nu-skool breaks

**Visual candidates:**

- *Nu Skool Breakz, Volume 1* compilation artwork;
- Plump DJs — *Electric Disco / Plumpy Chunks* twelve-inch;
- Stanton Warriors — *Da Antidote* artwork;
- a Friction at Bar Rumba flyer or photograph if a legible period scan is found.

**Primary listening example:** Plump DJs — Electric Disco

Spotify currently exposes the track through compilations; the original version should be selected rather than the later D. Ramirez remix.

Bandcamp release page with a directly playable original: https://plumpdjs.bandcamp.com/album/electric-disco-plumpy-chunks

**Secondary playlist track:** Stanton Warriors — Da Antidote

Use one compact player in the article. Place both tracks in the full listening playlist.

### 9. Contemporary breaks

**Primary feature video:** BICEP — Glue (Official Video)

YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A7ZxRs45tTg

This is more useful than a generic artist photo because the video revisits former rave locations and incorporates memories from people who attended those events. It supports both the contemporary section and the theme of rave memory.

**Alternative direct example:** Overmono — So U Kno

YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SRVxRUJxITY

Bandcamp: https://overmono.bandcamp.com/track/so-u-kno-1

Use Overmono as a compact audio example if Bicep is already the large feature video.

### 10. Breakbeat techno

**Primary listening example:** Skee Mask — 50 Euro to Break Boost

The track is a particularly defensible example because contemporary criticism explicitly describes the relationship between its breakbeat rhythm and techno scale. Use a Bandcamp or Spotify track embed when the final source is resolved.

Context source: https://pitchfork.com/reviews/tracks/skee-mask-50-euro-to-break-boost/

Verified Spotify album embed containing the track as number four: https://open.spotify.com/embed/album/3yXIkSJWpudtgF0TZuB16U

**Optional visual:** Ilian Tape release artwork or a small photograph of Munich's contemporary label ecosystem. Do not add a generic warehouse image.

### 11. Related styles comparison

Do not add five more large players. Use one compact comparison playlist, ordered to make the differences audible:

1. Jungle — Shy FX & UK Apache, Original Nuttah
2. Drum and bass — Photek, Ni-Ten-Ichi-Ryu or Goldie, Inner City Life
3. Big beat — The Chemical Brothers, Chemical Beats
4. Broken beat — Bugz in the Attic, Booty La La
5. Breakcore — Venetian Snares, Hajnal

The comparison table in the article should link each row to the matching playlist position where technically possible.

### 12. thecatrave integration

Place one SoundCloud mix after the contemporary section, not in the historical middle of the article.

Candidate: *I Like to Smoke in Silence After Raves*.

Before final insertion, check its tracklist against the surrounding statement. The CTA should explain its relevance:

### 13. Ending

Use a compact Bandcamp player or support CTA after the author/read-next block. It should not compete visually with the historical media or repeat the same message twice.

## Layout constraints

- Never place a large image directly against a large video.
- Place at least one meaningful paragraph between substantial media blocks.
- Archive flyers should be shown at a size that keeps their small text legible, but should not become full-viewport posters on desktop.
- Use paired artifacts in a two-column layout on desktop and stack them on mobile.
- Compact track players remain visually smaller than article headings and images.
- Use only two 16:9 feature videos in the full article.
- Do not shorten section text merely to force media into a rigid grid.
- Media captions should explain what the artifact proves or illustrates, not merely repeat its filename.
