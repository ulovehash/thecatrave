# The best techno mixes, from Detroit to now

## Answer

The best techno mixes show what a DJ does with the genre over time, not how hard one drop lands. Juan Atkins and Robert Hood make the Detroit continuum audible; Jeff Mills and Surgeon use fast, precise changes to keep machine rhythm alive; DJ Stingray pulls electro into the same conversation. Ben Klock, Helena Hauff, Wata Igarashi, Rødhåd and Fadi Mohem show how different the European and Japanese branches can sound. The ten recordings below come from official broadcaster channels.

## Introduction

A techno track may reveal itself in five minutes. A techno mix needs longer. Repetition creates the reference point, then a new hi-hat, bassline or patch of silence changes how the previous record sounded. The best DJs make those small changes feel physical.

This selection begins with Detroit artists and follows the music through industrial pressure, electro, Berlin club technique and hypnotic contemporary sets. It does not repeat the full history told in [What Is Techno?](/techno-music-guide). The subject here is the recorded mix: selection, sequence and control.

## What makes a techno mix essential?

Technical neatness is not enough. A mix earns another listen when the records form an argument, the energy has shape, and the DJ can change direction without losing the thread. That may mean Juan Atkins letting funk remain inside techno, Surgeon cutting between harder textures, or Wata Igarashi making small tonal shifts carry a mix.

The recordings are not ranked. They cover different uses and lineages, and each comes from the official YouTube channel of Mixmag, DJ Mag, Boiler Room or HÖR. View counts were not used as a quality score.

## Detroit: funk, minimalism and electro

### Juan Atkins, Mixmag Live, 2015

Juan Atkins plays techno as part of a longer Detroit machine-music continuum. The set keeps funk and electro close to the surface, which matters when later European recordings make the kick drum feel like the whole genre.

[Embed: Juan Atkins]

Begin here if the word techno currently means only dark rooms and straight pressure. Atkins leaves more air around the rhythm.

### Robert Hood, DJ Mag, 2019

Robert Hood's minimalism is severe without being empty. Short motifs and drum changes do the work that a breakdown would handle in a more theatrical set, and the groove keeps tightening through subtraction.

[Embed: Robert Hood]

The mix is a practical explanation of minimal techno. Fewer elements create more attention to timing, tone and what enters next.

### DJ Stingray, Boiler Room Dekmantel, 2017

DJ Stingray mixes electro at techno speed. Angular basslines, clipped snares and rapid transitions connect Detroit's Drexciyan strain to a harder contemporary floor without erasing the music's syncopation.

[Embed: DJ Stingray]

Choose this after Atkins to hear how the same city's machine funk becomes faster and more abrasive while keeping its broken rhythmic spine.

## Pressure and precision

### Jeff Mills, Mixmag, 2019

Jeff Mills treats the mixer and decks as instruments. Records arrive in fragments, lock together and disappear before their function turns obvious. The pace is quick, but the detail comes from how little time he needs to establish a new pattern.

[Embed: Jeff Mills]

The result stays lean. Mills keeps replacing information, which makes the set feel faster than its tempo alone would suggest.

### Surgeon, Boiler Room, 2014

Surgeon brings Birmingham's industrial weight into a set that still breathes. Distortion and dense drums are present, yet quieter transitions stop the pressure from settling into one grey block.

[Embed: Surgeon]

This is the route into harder techno for listeners who want texture and risk as well as force.

### Ben Klock, Boiler Room Berlin, 2013

Ben Klock's 2013 Berlin recording is built around long blends and controlled escalation. The records share a functional directness, but their low ends and percussion are spaced carefully enough that the set never sounds like one continuous loop.

[Embed: Ben Klock]

It captures a style of Berlin techno that depends on patience. The tension comes from waiting for a change, then noticing it has already happened.

## Different routes through contemporary techno

### Helena Hauff b2b L.F.T., HÖR, 2020

Helena Hauff and L.F.T. pull acid, electro, EBM and techno through the tiled HÖR booth. The back-to-back format gives the set a rougher conversational rhythm than a perfectly planned solo recording.

[Embed: Helena Hauff and L.F.T.]

The selections are wiry and physical. It is a useful counterweight to techno mixes that polish away every edge.

### Wata Igarashi, HÖR, 2023

Wata Igarashi works with depth, stereo movement and psychedelic repetition. Changes are gradual enough to feel continuous, but the atmosphere keeps shifting as percussion and resonant tones move forward or recede.

[Embed: Wata Igarashi]

This is the hypnotic choice in the list. It rewards attention to small adjustments.

### Rødhåd, Boiler Room, 2023

Rødhåd's set carries weight without rushing. The drums are broad and grounded, while dubby space around them keeps the mix from becoming flat at sustained intensity.

[Embed: Rodhad]

Play this for the long curve of a warehouse set, where the sustained mood carries more weight than any individual highlight.

### Fadi Mohem, Boiler Room Berlin, 2024

Fadi Mohem draws from dub techno, Detroit discipline and Berlin functionality. His transitions are direct, and the sound stays warm enough that the repeated patterns retain movement.

[Embed: Fadi Mohem]

The set closes the route because it gathers several earlier ideas without presenting them as retro references.

## Which techno mix should you play first?

Choose Juan Atkins for Detroit funk, Robert Hood for minimal techno and DJ Stingray for electro. Jeff Mills and Surgeon suit listeners who want faster decisions and harder texture. Ben Klock and Rødhåd take a longer Berlin route, Helena Hauff and L.F.T. add acid and EBM, Wata Igarashi goes deepest, and Fadi Mohem offers the most balanced current entry point.

There is no single best techno mix of all time. These essential techno mixes make different arguments about the genre, which is why the route works better unranked.
