## Semantic kernel

- Primary intent: explain what Movement Detroit is, where it happens, how it grew from DEMF and why its location in Detroit matters.
- Close secondary language: Movement Music Festival, Movement Detroit, Hart Plaza, Memorial Day weekend, Detroit techno festival, Movement history, Movement stages and Movement lineup.
- Required entities: Hart Plaza, Detroit River, Carol Marvin, Carl Craig, Derrick May, Kevin Saunderson, Paxahau, DEMF, Focus, Fuse-In, Detroit techno and the Belleville Three.
- Practical entities: six stages, Detroit Stage, Underground Stage, official app, accessibility, transport and official lineup link.
- FAQ candidates: What is Movement Detroit? Where is Movement held? What kind of music does Movement play? When does Movement happen? Is Movement only techno?
- Excluded intents: copying the current full lineup, ticket-price promises, hotel lists, unofficial afterparty recommendations, weather forecasts and a general Detroit travel guide.

## Proposed decision

- Recommendation: create a new evergreen page.
- SEO title: `Movement Detroit: Festival History, Location and Techno Legacy`
- Meta description: `Movement Detroit brings techno home to Hart Plaza each Memorial Day weekend. Learn the festival's history, location, stages and place in Detroit culture.`
- Visible H1: `Movement Detroit: techno in the city that made it`
- Title change from the earlier “History, Location and Techno Lineup” concept: remove `lineup` from the permanent metadata. Keep a dated current-edition box and a direct official-lineup link so the page remains accurate after every announcement cycle.

### Proposed outline

1. Direct answer: annual Memorial Day weekend electronic festival at Hart Plaza, rooted in Detroit techno but broader than techno alone.
2. Why Hart Plaza matters: the riverfront site, physical layout and connection to the city rather than generic festival scene-setting.
3. From DEMF to Movement: the 2000 launch, Focus years, Derrick May's Movement, Fuse-In and Paxahau's stewardship from 2006.
4. Detroit techno on its home ground: explain local programming and the festival's relationship to the artists and culture that predate it.
5. What music and stages to expect: techno, house, electro, ghettotech, hip-hop, breaks and experimental programming, using the official six-stage descriptions.
6. Current edition: a compact, dated information box with confirmed dates and direct official links, never a copied lineup that goes stale.
7. How to approach the weekend: official transport, accessibility and schedule resources, plus the role of city-wide parties without publishing an unverified list.
8. Essential Movement listening: exact historic or official festival sets placed beside the relevant history.
9. FAQ: retain the five durable questions above.

## Material uncertainties

- The official site has rolled forward to 2027, but not every current page exposes the same edition consistently. All year-specific facts need one final same-day check before publication.
- “Lineup” demand is valuable but volatile. The article should satisfy it with an official link and dated context, not copied names in permanent metadata.
- Festival history should state producer and naming changes precisely and avoid turning organizational disputes into a neat handoff story.
- Rights for Detroit Historical Society stills are not assumed. Video embedding or citation may be possible even when image reuse is not.
- Exact historical sets and CC image licences remain to be verified before drafting and implementation.
