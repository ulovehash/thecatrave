# Bass Music Guide — competitor media audit and original media plan

Updated: 2026-08-31

## Editorial decision

The article should not imitate the weak visual pattern of most pages ranking for broad `bass music` queries. Those pages rely on generic festival portraits, stock studio photographs or a sequence of artist thumbnails. Our page should work as a scene-led visual history: each image must establish a place, an infrastructure or an artefact that the surrounding paragraph discusses.

The visual story must be global. Britain remains important because the loose umbrella term became especially visible in late-2000s UK club writing, but the media sequence must give independent visual weight to Jamaica, Miami, Los Angeles, Chicago and Durban.

## Recommended media sequence

### 1. Hero: `One term, different histories`

**Format:** an original branded editorial graphic rather than a borrowed festival photograph.

**Content:** six independent scene nodes — Kingston, Miami, Britain, Los Angeles, Chicago and Durban — connected only by documented routes such as migration, records, touring, radio, labels and digital exchange. It must not look like a false genre family tree.

**Editorial job:** communicate the article’s central argument before the reader reaches the first historical section. This is also the strongest backlink/share asset.

### 2. Jamaica: King Tubby’s actual MCI console

**Preferred source:** [Museum of Pop Culture object record](https://mopop.emuseum.com/objects/95703/mci-mixing-console-formerly-owned-and-operated-by-king-tubby?idx=5), which provides three images and identifies a 300 dpi asset.

**Alternative context source:** [Philharmonie de Paris — Sound the System](https://deneb.philharmoniedeparis.fr/uploads/documents/60a631706ee59_JAMAICA%20ENG.pdf), where the console is presented as a central historical object.

**Treatment:** make a clean transparent cutout of the console only if the original resolution supports it. Do not fabricate missing edges. Pair it with the paragraph explaining the studio as an instrument, not directly beside the King Tubby player.

**Why this is new:** no current article uses this object. It is more specific than another generic sound-system stack.

### 3. Jamaica: one archival scene photograph

**Preferred source:** [The Vinyl Factory’s 1975–76 Jamaica archive feature](https://archive.thevinylfactory.com/features/from-studios-to-sound-systems-inside-jamaica-reggae-scene-during-75-76/).

**Candidate subject:** King Tubby testing amplifiers, Lee Perry at Black Ark or a Kingston record-shop/sound-system scene. Select one, not a gallery dump.

**Editorial job:** establish that sound-system culture involved technicians, shops, streets and crews, not only famous producers.

### 4. Miami: mobile DJ speaker wall, not a generic TR-808

**Preferred source:** [RBMA Daily’s Miami mobile DJ oral history](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2019/09/miami-bass-mobile-djs-regulating-oral-history/).

**Candidate:** the archival Loc Ace and Vic photograph in front of the speaker wall, or the Chipman mobile-DJ scene. The source also documents Pac Jam, regulating, speaker builders and community events.

**Implemented homepage treatment, 1 September 2026:** the Loc Ace and Vic photograph is the Bass Music article-card thumbnail. Local responsive files are stored at `img/bass-music/miami-bass-loc-ace-vic-480.jpg` and `img/bass-music/miami-bass-loc-ace-vic-1400.jpg`. The branded global-history SVG remains inside the article because it explains the page's parallel-history argument, but it is too diagrammatic for the compact homepage card.

**Alternative artefact:** the [Miami Fresh Festival Globe poster](https://jointcustodydc.com/products/vintage-miami-fresh-festival-m-c-cool-rock-m-c-chaszy-chess-ice-man-ja-dynamix-ii-baron-davis-and-the-fresh-crew-globe-poster-printing-corp-show-poster), which includes Dynamix II and other regional bass acts.

**Editorial job:** show Miami bass as a South Florida social and sound-system history that predates American dubstep.

### 5. UK umbrella moment: a 2009 artefact, not another rave crowd

**Preferred candidate:** [Bok Bok / L-Vis 1990 — Night Slugs EP](https://boomkat.com/products/night-slugs-ep-5decf68d-b621-4d95-977c-691671762a26), released in 2009 before the Night Slugs label launch and described contemporaneously through garage, house, dubstep, funky and Baltimore-club language.

**Alternative:** a verified early Night Slugs party flyer from 2008–10 with an adequate original scan.

**Editorial job:** give the reader a physical artefact from the period when the umbrella terminology became useful. Do not use Skream, a generic UKF image or another anonymous rave flyer.

### 6. Los Angeles: local room and system

**Source:** [LA Weekly’s history of Los Angeles bass music](https://www.laweekly.com/a-history-of-bass-music-in-los-angeles/).

**Preferred candidate:** Pure Filth’s speaker blocks or Nocando at Low End Theory. Both explain local infrastructure better than the overused Skrillex performance image.

**Fallback:** an early Low End Theory / Airliner flyer with a verifiable date and a high-resolution scan.

**Editorial job:** make the LA section visibly local rather than illustrating it with a British dubstep artist touring America.

### 7. Chicago: footwork as dance culture

**Preferred source:** [NPR’s Wills Glasspiegel footwork report](https://www.npr.org/sections/therecord/2011/05/11/136209254/footwork-chicago-dance-music-with-a-need-for-speed).

**Candidate:** the dance-battle image, or the group portrait of DJ Clent, Bobby Skillz, DJ Rashad, DJ Spinn, Gantman, Patricia and Traxman in front of the Chicago Picasso.

**Editorial job:** prevent the article from treating footwork as a rhythm imported into UK records. The visual must foreground dancers and the Chicago network.

### 8. Durban: circulation through people, taxis and house parties

**Preferred source:** [DJ Mag — Recognise: DJ Lag](https://djmag.com/content/recognise-dj-lag), whose reporting connects early house parties, taxi sound systems and later clubs.

**Image candidates:** a documented Durban taxi/sound-system scene, or the community image of Distruction Boyz around the corrugated rehearsal space from [Afropop Worldwide](https://www.afropop.org/articles/distruction-boyz). A generic DJ-on-stage portrait is the fallback, not the first choice.

**Editorial job:** visualise the local circulation system described in the text and avoid presenting gqom as a UK-discovered export.

### 9. Contemporary section: thecatrave mixes, no extra decorative photograph

Embed these as two separate full-width SoundCloud listening stripes in different sections:

1. `I Lost So Many Weekends Raving and I Wanna Lose Some More` in the infrastructure section. Its editorial role is the DJ mix as a modern distribution and sequencing format.
2. `I Like to Smoke in Silence After Raves` in the contemporary-hybrids section. Its editorial role is a personal example of connecting breaks, bass, garage pressure and experimental club music without claiming they are one genre.

Do not place an image immediately before or after either player. Each player must interrupt a substantial run of prose and include one short sentence explaining why the mix belongs at that exact point.

## Deliberately rejected media

- Another Skrillex hero or performance photograph: recognisable but collapses global history into the US dubstep boom.
- Generic festival crowd shots: atmosphere without historical evidence.
- Stock DAW, synthesizer or mixing-desk photographs: they shift the page toward a production tutorial intent.
- Reused Roland TR-808 photograph: the instrument matters to Miami bass, but the existing site already leans heavily on isolated hardware. The Miami speaker-wall archive tells the more distinctive story.
- A row of twelve record covers: useful as a compact recap only, but inferior to playable tracks placed in the relevant narrative sections.
- A false global genre family tree: it would imply descent where the article argues for parallel histories and exchange.

## Image and layout acceptance rules

- Never upscale a source. Reject any image that cannot remain sharp at its intended desktop width.
- Preferred source original: at least 1600 px on the long edge for full-width images; at least 1000 px for narrower editorial figures.
- Produce responsive derivatives at approximately 480, 960 and 1600 px where the source permits; use `srcset`, `sizes`, explicit `width` and `height`, `loading="lazy"` and `decoding="async"`.
- Preserve the native aspect ratio. CSS must use `width: 100%; height: auto;`. Cropping is editorial and must be set deliberately with a fixed aspect-ratio wrapper, never caused by accidental stretching.
- Transparent cutouts are reserved for actual objects such as the King Tubby console. Documentary photographs and flyers keep their photographic/paper context.
- Images and players are pacing devices. Never stack image → player or player → image without meaningful prose between them.
- Captions explain what the image proves in the story; they do not repeat the paragraph or show raw licensing notes in the visible article.
