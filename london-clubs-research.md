# Stage 6 verdict, 2026-09-11

Separate validation pass over the evidence above. The pass gathered nothing and
ruled on what it was given, per `TOPIC-RESEARCH.md`.

**Verdict: write something narrower — a history-led guide to London's clubs,
titled and URL'd for `clubs in london`, whose subject is the rooms that made the
music, with a short, criterion-bound table of the music-led venues still open,
not a twelve-venue best-of.**

**The number that matters is 3,300 (`clubs in london`), not 2,500 and not the
"~15,000" this file used to claim.** The sum was a de-duplication error: one
query typed many ways, which stage 5 itself proves by showing a single URL
ranking across the whole list.

**Why not a best-of.** Against the position-2 page — Condé Nast Traveller, a
London editorial desk that goes out on the magazine's money — our version of
"twelve venues for this weekend" would be *similar and worse*, which
`KEYWORD-METHOD.md` §5 says to treat as a warning. It also takes on a permanent
maintenance debt (the status column, revisited every six months) that this
package never costed. Stated once: the differentiated sections are not what the
query wants, and the section the query wants is not differentiated. So the
history and the owner's own standing are the reason to read the page, and the venue
table is present because the SERP requires it, not because it is the product.

**What the top ten actually is:** eight organic slots, every one an article — two
magazine listicles, two institutional guides, two guestlist/VIP funnels wearing
an article's clothes, one UGC aggregate, one content-marketing filler. No forum,
platform or Wikipedia wall. Google is asking for an article, but specifically
for a maintained inventory of open venues, which is a genre this site has never
written.

   "Stage 4, completed after the verdict". Original note: Stage 5 says "that is the shape

## Candidate ranking

**1. London. 2. Manchester / the Haçienda — on standing, not on opportunity.**
The Haçienda has been through none of stages 2–5 as its own topic; two rows of
volume and one incidentally-pulled SERP have no standing against five stages,
and saying otherwise is a guess wearing a number. On the visible evidence the
pass expects the Haçienda to overtake London — 3,500 on a single non-collision
term, four history PAAs about a venue closed since 1997, a 0-RD page answering
them at position 6, no local pack, nothing to maintain — held back by an AI
Overview, Wikipedia at 2, and only two editorial slots.

What would change the ranking: run stages 2–5 on the Haçienda (if the question
forms give two or more editorial slots under ~10 RD and the AI Overview is not
swallowing the click, it goes first); if `clubs in london` comes back owned by
ticketing and listings platforms, London's number is not addressable and it
drops; if the
owner's standing (§5) turns out Manchester-weighted, that reverses it alone.
Domain rating changes nothing either way.

## Ruling on the two-audience tension

**The entities appear, the recommendations do not, and the criterion is stated
out loud in the first hundred words.** A room is here because of what plays in
it, not because you can book a table in it. Cirque le Soir, Cuckoo Club, Home
House and Ballie Ballerson are named **once, in a single paragraph of real
prose**, as the other London — the bottle-service and hen-night city most of
this SERP is selling — with a plain sentence saying this guide is not about it
and that the reader who wants it is better served by the guestlist sites above
us. That puts the `also_talk_about` entities on the page so Google reads it as
belonging to the set, and sends the wrong reader away in ten seconds instead of
at section four.

Two consequences accepted up front: this page will not take position 1–2 for
`best clubs in london` and must not be scoped as if it might; and that paragraph
must be prose with a point of view, not four venue names in a row — written the
second way it is keyword stuffing with an alibi, which `WRITING.md` already
closes.

## Facts corrected by the verification pass

- **Trip:** end of May 1988 (Nicky Holloway, London Astoria). Acid house says
  June 1988; Shoom's article says June 1987 and is outvoted. The page names the
  disagreement.
- **Corsica Studios:** closing in its current form announced September 2025
  (Wikipedia); RA records the last night on 28 March. Listed as closed.
- **The Cause:** Tottenham Hale 2018 to 2022, 60 Dock Road, Silvertown from 2023.
- **Printworks:** closed for redevelopment in 2023 (ICMP's 2022 is not used);
  Press Halls scheduled to reopen in 2026, not asserted as reopened.
- **Drumsheds:** Edmonton (Time Out says Tottenham, after the IKEA store's name),
  capacity 15,000.
- **Rage and jungle:** now two sources. Heaven's article names Rage with Fabio &
  Grooverider; Metalheadz's says Kemistry took Goldie to Rage and it shaped him.
- **The End:** opened 2 December 1995, closed 2009 (ICMP's 2008 is not used).
