# Drum & Bass guide — fact-check notes and sources

Companion to `dnb-guide-research.md` and `drum-and-bass-guide-draft.md`. Every claim used in the draft that is a date, attribution, quote or "first/largest" statement is listed here with its source and a confidence note. Anything not verifiable to a reputable source is written in the draft as inference or attributed opinion, per `ARTICLE-PRODUCTION-WORKFLOW.md` §2 and `AGENTS.md` §110.

Research date: 2 September 2026.

## The split (prologue)

- **Metalheadz founded 1994** by Goldie with Kemistry & Storm. Label press language: it aimed to "explore both the roots of Breakbeat and Jungle, and rework it into a new dimension, Drum 'n' Bass." — Wikipedia *Metalheadz*; Beatportal, *Beatport's Definitive History of Drum & Bass*. Confidence: high.
- **Goldie, *Timeless*, released 7 August 1995** (FFRR / London). Widely treated as the record that made the "drum & bass" ambition legible to outsiders. — Wikipedia *Timeless (Goldie album)*; 909originals interview July 2020 ("My job is to make breakbeat more acceptable"). Confidence: high on date; the "made it legible" framing is editorial.
- **The transition was gradual (roughly 1993–1996), not an event.** Partly a distancing from ragga jungle's public image, partly an aesthetic move toward jazz-inflected, more linear production. — Beatportal; Wikipedia *Drum and bass* ("by 1995 some jungle producers moved away from ragga-influenced styles"). Draft must present this as contested/gradual, not a clean break.
- **Speed** (club night, Mars Bar, London, 1994–95) run by **Fabio and LTJ Bukem**, focused on the jazz-influenced, mellower side. — Beatportal; Wikipedia. Confidence: high.
- **Rage** (Heaven, London, run by Fabio & Grooverider, roughly 1988–1993) is the earlier breakbeat-hardcore night usually cited as where the sound formed; belongs to the jungle guide's territory, referenced only in passing. Confidence: high.
- The racial / class / commercialisation reading of the rename, and the "violence at raves drove promoters away" account (Marcus Intalex biography), are **already covered in the jungle guide's `#myths`**. The D&B draft summarises in one or two sentences and links out; it does not re-argue them.

## How the sound is built

- **Tempo usually 160–180 BPM, predominantly 170–180 since ~1996; ~174 is the de facto standard.** Historical drift: ~130 BPM (1990–91) → ~155–165 (1993) → 170–180 (from ~1996). — Wikipedia *Drum and bass*. Confidence: high for the range; "174 as standard" is common knowledge in the scene, stated as such.
- **Amen break** (from The Winstons' "Amen, Brother", 1969) and other funk breaks, timestretched and rearranged, are the rhythmic core. The break/timestretch technique itself is the breakbeat guide's subject — link out, don't re-explain. Confidence: high.
- **"Two-step" vs "rolling"** as the two dominant drum feels, and **sub-bass / Reese bass** (a detuned-saw bass named after Kevin Saunderson's "Reese" alias, via "Just Another Chance", 1988) as the low-end vocabulary. Confidence: high on the terms; the Reese etymology is well documented but keep it a half-sentence.
- **Comparison with dubstep:** D&B runs at full tempo ~174 with busy breakbeats; dubstep sits at ~140 with half-time drums. Both are bass-led. This is a factual contrast, not a value judgement. Links to `/dubstep-guide`.

## Metalheadz and the dark turn

- **Metalheadz Sunday Sessions at the Blue Note, Hoxton Square, London (1995 onward)** — the residency most associated with the label's peak. Confidence: high (widely documented); verify the exact run of years before stating them precisely, otherwise say "from 1995".
- **Techstep**: harder, machine-led direction from **No U-Turn Records** (Nico Sykes' studio/label), artists **Ed Rush, Nico, Trace, Fierce, Ryme Tyme**; the ***Torque*** compilation (No U-Turn, 1997) is the landmark. The term "techstep" is usually credited to **Trace, Ed Rush and Nico** / the No U-Turn circle and popularised via Grooverider's radio play; a Japanese writer, **Kensuke Shiina**, is sometimes credited with coining it in print. Draft: attribute loosely ("the name is usually traced to…"), do not assert a single coiner. — Beatportal; Wikipedia *Drum and bass*. Confidence: medium on coinage, high on the No U-Turn/Torque facts.
- **Ed Rush & Optical** founded **Virus Recordings (1998)** and released **the album *Wormhole* (1998)**, a pivot point from techstep toward what became neurofunk. — Beatportal. Confidence: high.

## Neurofunk

- **Neurofunk** = the funk/jazz-informed evolution of techstep: sub-bass replaced by "snarling midrange", heavy sound-design, precise editing. — Wikipedia *Drum and bass*; Beatportal ("replaced the pressurized sub-bass of jungle with snarling midrange"). Confidence: high for the description.
- The **term "neurofunk"** is commonly credited to writer **Kodwo Eshin** in *More Brilliant Than the Sun* (1998), describing the No U-Turn / Virus sound. Draft: attribute to Eshin's book, note it was a critic's coinage. Confidence: medium-high — verify the exact wording/page before quoting; if not verified, say "coined by the writer Kodwo Eshun" without a quote.
- **Noisia** (Groningen, Netherlands) and **Black Sun Empire** carried neurofunk internationally in the 2000s. — Beatportal. Confidence: high.

## The atmospheric line

- **LTJ Bukem**, **Good Looking Records** (founded 1990/91), the **Logical Progression** compilation series (first volume 1996) and the **Speed** night define the mellow, jazz/ambient-facing branch. Bukem's "Music" (1993, on Good Looking) is the usual reference track. — Beatportal; Wikipedia. Confidence: high on the entities; verify Good Looking's founding year and "Music" release year individually before printing exact dates.
- **Roni Size / Reprazent, *New Forms* (Talkin' Loud, 1997) won the Mercury Prize 1997**, beating Radiohead's *OK Computer*, The Prodigy and Spice Girls among others. — Beatportal; Wikipedia; widely reported. Confidence: high.
- **Liquid funk**: **Fabio's mix CD *Liquid Funk* (Creative Source, 2000)** is the usual origin point of the name; later carried by **High Contrast, Calibre, Marcus Intalex (Soul:r)**, London Elektricity / **Hospital Records**. — Beatportal. Confidence: high on Fabio 2000; "usual origin point" is the honest hedge.

## Subgenres (for the table — 1 line each, relationship stated)

| Term | Roughly when | What it is | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drum and bass (the core) | 1994 onward | ~174 BPM, breakbeats, sub-bass; the form jungle became | The source |
| Liquid / liquid funk | 2000 onward | Melodic, soulful, two-step rolls; vocals and chords | The mellow line, named after Fabio's 2000 mix |
| Jump-up | mid-1990s onward | Big, cartoonish basslines, simple breaks, party function | Split early from "breakbeat science"; DJ Zinc, Aphrodite |
| Techstep | 1996–99 | Machine-led, industrial, sci-fi; No U-Turn / *Torque* | The hard turn away from jazz |
| Neurofunk | late 1990s onward | Techstep plus funk precision and mid-range design | Descends from techstep; Ed Rush & Optical, Noisia |
| Darkstep / drumfunk | late 1990s onward | Very fast dark drums / dense edited breaks | Wings of the same tree; drumfunk is a Photek/Paradox lineage |
| Halftime | 2010s onward | D&B sub-bass and sound design at a half-time drum feel (~85–87 felt) | The point where D&B and dubstep tempo logic meet |

Verify DJ Zinc "Super Sharp Shooter" (1995) / "Pranksters" and Aphrodite dates individually if named in the draft.

## How it stopped being British

- **Brazil / "sambass":** DJ **Marky**, **XRS**, **Patife**, connected to the UK through **Bryan Gee's V Recordings**; Marky's international breakout is usually dated to around 2001–2002 ("LK" with XRS, 2002). — Beatportal. Confidence: high on the names/label; verify "LK" year.
- **North America:** Toronto cited as the strongest early non-UK scene; **1.8.7 / Jordana LeSesne** an early US producer. — Beatportal. Confidence: medium — treat as "often cited", not definitive.
- **Festivals:** UK **Hospitality** events; **Rampage** (Antwerp, Belgium) as the largest indoor D&B event; **Let It Roll** (Czech Republic) as the largest open-air; **Pirate Station** (Russia) described in 2009 as "the world's largest drum and bass festival". Draft: attribute each superlative ("billed as", "described as"), never assert. Confidence: medium — these claims shift year to year.

## Where it is now

- **Streams up ~94% between 2021 and 2024**, per **Spotify** (reported by DJ Mag, 2024); majority of listeners under 34. — DJ Mag, "Drum & bass streams increased 94% in the past three years, Spotify reports"; Wikipedia. Attribute to Spotify, not "the industry".
- **Pitchfork** noted a "rising zoomer affinity" for the genre in 2021. — Wikipedia *Drum and bass*. Attribute to Pitchfork.
- **Nia Archives** is generally regarded as at the forefront of the post-2020 revival; album **_Silence Is Loud_ (2024)** peaked at **No. 16 on the UK Albums Chart**. — Wikipedia *Nia Archives*, *Silence Is Loud*. Confidence: high.

## FAQ answers — factual basis

- **What does DnB stand for:** drum and bass (also written d&b, drum & bass, drum'n'bass). Not the bank or Dun & Bradstreet — that is a separate use of the acronym. — trivially verifiable.
- **BPM:** 170–180, ~174 standard (see above).
- **Same as jungle:** shared 1991–94 root, tempo and drum vocabulary; jungle names the breakbeat-and-reggae scene of 1991–95, drum and bass names what much of it became. Link jungle guide.
- **Is it EDM:** predates the US "EDM" marketing term (which took hold around 2010–12) and developed separately in the UK; the two now share festival bills. — general knowledge; frame as "predates and sits apart from".
- **Subgenres:** liquid, jump-up, neurofunk, techstep, plus darkstep / drumfunk / halftime.
